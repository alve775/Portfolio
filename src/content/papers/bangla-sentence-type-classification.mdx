abstract: 'Transformer-based models such as BERT have significantly advanced natural language processing, yet low-resource languages like Bangla remain underexplored despite being spoken by over 230 million people. Grammatical sentence-type classification—covering assertive, interrogative, imperative, exclamatory, and optative forms—is essential for applications such as dialogue systems and machine translation but lacks a standardized benchmark in Bangla NLP. To address this gap, we introduce a balanced dataset of 25,500 manually annotated Bangla sentences and compare three monolingual models (BanglaBERT Base, BanglaBERT, SahajBERT) with three cross-lingual models (XLM-RoBERTa, mBERT, RemBERT) under identical settings. Our study evaluates both accuracy and computational efficiency, highlighting the trade-offs between language-specific pretraining and multilingual transfer learning while establishing a reproducible benchmark for Bangla sentence-type classification.'
takeaway: "On the five-class Bangla sentence benchmark, the best monolingual and multilingual encoders reached similar test accuracy. This comparison supports evaluating both model families for the task; the small observed gap does not establish general monolingual superiority."
