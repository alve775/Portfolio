abstract: "This undergraduate thesis examines temporal and regional EEG candidate discovery in a public cohort of 121 children. It combines participant-level nested cross-validation, a fixed 60-second interval, duration-and-quality and regional spectral-power controls, paired bootstrap inference, multiplicity correction, and shared-label permutation checks. Raw and FIR-filtered EEG form the primary comparison, with ASR and endpoint correction used for sensitivity analyses. The results identify preprocessing-sensitive sensor-space associations and a leading central–parietal coherence candidate. Independent cohorts and prospective evaluation are still needed before clinical interpretation."
takeaway: "Subject-level validation and fold-local processing prevent the same participant from contributing to both training and evaluation. Central–parietal coherence was the leading localized candidate after regional-power control, but its adjusted result did not establish family-wise confirmation."
